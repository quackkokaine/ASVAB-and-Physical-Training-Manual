This page follows the Helmsman booklet published by Training Center Cape May (the PDF is dated May 2017). Fitness standards, packing lists and phone numbers can change, so confirm them with your recruiter before you go.

Eight weeks

Recruit training is eight weeks long. You can be held back longer for disciplinary, medical, academic or physical fitness reasons.

Bank account and ATM card

Your pay is direct deposited, and the only way to reach it is with an ATM card. Try the card at least once before you arrive.

Bring ID

You must bring your Social Security card and at least one form of photo ID. Bring about $50 in small bills.

Contraband

Contraband is thrown away, and some of it gets you removed from training right away. See the list in the packing section.

No personal phone calls

You cannot receive personal calls during recruit training. Call your family from the Philadelphia USO when you arrive.

You are under the UCMJ

Lying, cheating or stealing will not be tolerated. If you make a mistake, own up to it.

Before you arrivePrepare yourself physically, mentally and financially. Make sure you also have the right clothing and supplies, and most of all the right attitude.

Financial arrangements Before you go

  • Arrange with your creditors either to delay payments for two months or to pay in advance for the time you are away.
  • At Cape May you must have a checking or savings account and an ATM card. Pay is direct deposited, and an ATM card is the only way to get it. Try your card at least once before you arrive.
  • More about pay is in the Pay and finances section.
Why it matters

If you do not have a bank account and ATM card, your pay will be seriously disrupted, and you would have to spend training time sorting it out.

What to bring Before you go

Personal items fall into three categories:

Issue

Items issued to you when you arrive at the Training Center.

Permitted

Items you are allowed to bring into training.

Contraband

Items strictly forbidden. They are thrown away.

  • You must bring your Social Security card and at least one form of photo ID. Bring about $50 in small bills.
  • You may bring one small suitcase or duffel bag, no larger than an airline carry-on, with your name printed clearly on it. It stores the clothes you arrive in until graduation.
  • Arrive in comfortable, weather-appropriate clothes and sneaker-type shoes.

Physical fitness training Before you go

The recruits who succeed in fitness training all have one thing in common: they started preparing early.

Getting in shape

  • Doctor exam: if you have never exercised regularly, get a physical exam first to make sure it is safe.
  • Clothing: dress for the temperature. In the cold, cover your ears and fingers. In the heat, wear cool cotton clothing.
  • Footwear: wear a good quality running sneaker.

The elements of fitness

  • Stop using tobacco.
  • If you are 21 or older, moderate your alcohol use.
  • Follow a low-fat nutrition plan.
  • Follow an exercise plan that includes cardiorespiratory endurance, muscular fitness, flexibility and strength.
Tobacco

Smoking and chewing tobacco are prohibited in recruit training, and the first few weeks are tough enough already. Make every effort to be tobacco free before you arrive. A smoking cessation program is highly recommended if you are a heavy smoker or have smoked for several years.

Stretching

Stretch before strenuous exercise and again as part of your cool-down. Do not bounce. Slowly extend and hold each stretch for 10 to 15 seconds.

Bent-knee sit-ups

Minimum to graduate

Men: 38 in one minute. Women: 32 in one minute.

This measures abdominal muscular endurance. Lie on your back with knees bent, feet flat on the deck and hands cupped behind your ears. Have someone hold your feet if needed. Keep your lower back on the deck and raise your upper body about 90 degrees, touching your elbows to your knees, then lower with control. Once your shoulder blades touch the deck, you can come up again. Do not tuck your chin into your chest, which strains your neck.

Push-ups

Minimum to graduate

Men: 29 in one minute. Women: 15 in one minute.

This measures upper body muscular endurance. Place your hands shoulder-width apart with your feet together and your body in a straight line from shoulders to ankles. Keep it straight the whole time. Lower until your upper arms are at least parallel to the ground, then push back up until your arms are fully straight.

Running

Minimum to graduate

1.5 mile run: men 12:51, women 15:26.

  • This measures full-body endurance. Stretch thoroughly first, wear properly fitting running shoes, and pick a good surface (a track, or a level street).
  • Relax and breathe properly. Keep your elbows close to your sides to save energy, and lengthen your stride to reduce stress on your joints.
  • When you tire, slow to a brisk walk and keep pumping your arms to hold an aerobic heart rate.
  • If you have not run for a long time, start with a walk/run program. For example, walk five minutes then run two minutes, repeating three times, for 21 minutes total. Do aerobic exercise three to five times a week.

Swimming

Minimum to graduate

Jump off a six-foot platform into the pool, swim 100 meters, and tread water for five minutes. Swimming on your back is not permitted.

A good swim training program has seven parts: stretching, warm-up, kick/pull or drill sets, a major set, a timed swim, sprints and a warm-down. You can find resources online to design a program for your swim qualification.

Your arrival

Getting to Cape May Travel

  • Start your travel well rested. Your first night you sleep in the clothes you arrive in, so travel comfortably.
  • At Philadelphia International Airport, all recruits must report immediately to the USO office on the secure side (not the ticket counter side) of Terminal A-East. A USO volunteer will help with the rest of your trip.
  • If your flight is late, or you have any other problem, call Training Center Cape May at 1-609-898-6971.

Keys to success

How to succeed at training

Act with honor

You are now subject to the Uniform Code of Military Justice (UCMJ). Lying, cheating or stealing will not be tolerated. If you do something wrong, do not hide it or lie. Own up to it. Violating our core values can have serious consequences.

Take things seriously

The staff is serious about training you to be an effective member of the armed forces. If you make light of what your Company Commander is teaching, you will face stern discipline. This is not the place for jokes.

Build up your condition

Some recruits find the fitness program easier than their usual routine, and many find it very difficult. Follow the fitness advice above, get in shape, and build up slowly so you do not get hurt.

Follow orders

You will learn to obey all orders quickly, willingly and without question. Get used to it. It helps in training and in your Coast Guard career.

Our Core Values are Honor (integrity is our standard), Respect (we treat each other and those we serve with fairness, dignity and compassion) and Devotion to Duty (we seek responsibility, accept accountability and serve with pride).

Required knowledgeThe required knowledge is mandatory. The more you memorize before you arrive, the better off you will be.

Eleven General Orders Memorize

The Eleven General Orders are common to all branches of the U.S. Armed Forces.

  1. To take charge of this post and all government property in view.
  2. To walk my post in a military manner, keeping always on the alert, and observing everything that takes place within sight or hearing.
  3. To report all violations of orders I am instructed to enforce.
  4. To repeat all calls from posts more distant from the guard house than my own.
  5. To quit my post only when properly relieved.
  6. To receive, obey and pass on to the sentry who relieves me all orders from the commanding officer, field officer of the day, officer of the day, and officers and petty officers of the watch.
  7. To talk to no one except in the line of duty.
  8. To give the alarm in case of fire or disorder.
  9. To call the petty officer of the watch in any case not covered by instructions.
  10. To salute all officers and all colors and standards not cased.
  11. To be especially watchful at night, and during the time for challenging, to challenge all persons on or near my post, and to allow no one to pass without proper authority.

Attention, salutes, and addressing personnel Memorize

The position of attention

The purpose is to build military bearing and self-discipline. Stand like this:

  • Smartly bring your left heel against the right.
  • Turn your feet out equally to form a 45 degree angle, heels on the same line and touching.
  • Keep your legs straight but not stiff at the knees.
  • Keep your hips and shoulders level and your chest lifted.
  • Let your arms hang naturally, thumbs along the trouser seams, palms facing in, fingers joined in their natural curl.
  • Keep your head and body erect and look straight ahead. Keep your mouth closed and your chin pulled in slightly.
  • Stand still and do not talk.

Salutes

A salute is always accompanied by a proper greeting, such as "Sir, Good Morning, Sir" or "Ma'am, Good Afternoon, Ma'am." It is military tradition that recognizes the other person's seniority.

Addressing military personnel

  • Address all personnel with "Sir" or "Ma'am" at the beginning and the end of the sentence or phrase.
  • Assume the position of attention when speaking to, or being addressed by, any permanent party member.
  • "Carry on" may be granted only by the person in authority, and only if the recruit shows proper military bearing.

Military time Memorize

Military time numbers the hours 00 to 23, so a.m. and p.m. are not needed. Midnight is 00, 1 a.m. is 01, 1 p.m. is 13, and so on. Minutes and seconds are the same as regular time.

Know your chain of command Learn

A chain of command is a structure of authority and responsibility, so everyone knows their place and to whom they report. Your immediate supervisors are your Company Commander (CC) and Lead Company Commander (LCC). Their supervisor is the Section Commander (SC), and it continues up to the President. You are required to know each person's rank and name. Your Company Commander will tell you when to fill them in.

  1. Commander-in-Chief (the President of the United States)
  2. Secretary of Homeland Security
  3. Coast Guard Commandant
  4. Force Readiness Command

  6. Executive Officer (XO)

Also good to know, though not in your chain of command: the Command Master Chief (CMC), the Chaplain, and the Equal Opportunity Advisor.

Charting your courseAn eight-week journey. Training is tough because the work is important: the Coast Guard saves lives and enforces the law.

Stages of training

Stress is on purpose

It makes sure you have what it takes to defend your country, enforce our laws and save people in difficult circumstances. As you progress, you are expected to perform at higher and higher levels. Basic training is eight weeks, but you can be held back for disciplinary, medical or academic reasons.

1. Arrival

Forming begins. You go through medical screening and administrative processing, and receive your uniforms and a haircut. During forming week you may not feel like a military member, but you begin to look and act like one.

2. Company Formation

After forming week you are assigned to a Recruit Training Company and meet your Company Commanders. Your first weekend with the company is challenging: you learn to act as a team member and to obey orders. Many recruits find it the hardest stretch of boot camp. Your goal is to get "squared away" and begin your transformation.

3. Learning the Basics

An introduction to physical fitness and required knowledge. You start military drill, customs and courtesies, and basic Coast Guard orientation. The more you memorize before you arrive, the better.

4. Practical Training

More hands-on: fire fighting, marksmanship, line handling and seamanship, plus classroom instruction. At the end of week four you take the mid-term exam. When you pass, you complete your Assignment Data Card (ADC). Recruit assignments are limited to district only. The Coast Guard assigns you to a ship or shore unit based on its needs, and tries to match your preferences. Most recruits find their first duty is operational. You must contact your new unit and talk to a designated sponsor for the information you need.

5. Preparation for Your First Unit

At the end of week five you receive orders to your first assignment. Training covers first aid and watch standing, and you learn about your military entitlements to prepare for travel and graduation. All details are handled before you leave Cape May.

6. Graduation

The culmination of recruit training. Ceremonies are usually held Friday morning at 1100, outdoors, or in the Training Center gymnasium in bad weather. Your family is sent a letter with the details, and is always invited and encouraged to attend.

Pay and finances

Pay, housing and education Money

  • ATM: ATMs are located at Dempsey Hall and in the Exchange.
  • Pay grade: recruits are paid at the current scale for the pay grade they enlisted in. Most enlist as E-1, though some programs start at E-2 or E-3.
  • Basic Allowance for Housing (BAH): recruits with families may apply for BAH, normally during week one. The amount depends on your dependents.
  • Tuition assistance: up to $4,500 is available each fiscal year.
  • Montgomery GI Bill: you are automatically enrolled at enlistment. During week 3 you can choose to disenroll.
  • Post 9/11 GI Bill: you earn this benefit by serving honorably for 36 months. The two programs differ in important ways, so learn which one fits your education plans.
  • Reserve members: the Montgomery GI Bill (Selected Reserve) and the Reserve Education Assistance Program (REAP) are available.

Learn more at www.va.gov or ask your recruiter.

General information

Counseling, chapel and medical Support

Training is very strenuous, and being away from family and friends can create a need for counseling. You are never alone, and someone is always available to help.

  • Start with your Company Commander. Your CC has experience with problems like yours.
  • If you are uncomfortable talking to your CC, you can see a chaplain or another counseling resource: a staff psychiatrist, the equal opportunity advisor, a social worker or a staff nurse. Appointments are made through your Company Commander.
  • Chapel: Catholic and Protestant chaplains hold services every Sunday morning. Arrangements can be made for other services available in the community if they do not conflict with training. You may also join the recruit choir. Two recruits per company serve as Company Religious Representatives.
  • Medical: the Training Center Dispensary has family physicians, physical therapy, a dental clinic, optometry and a psychiatrist for mild to moderate illness, including overnight treatment. Emergency room services are at local hospitals.

Relationships and liberty Rules

Recruits may not socialize with

Cape May training staff (non-recruit military and civilian personnel), and recruits in other companies that are not in the same week of training.

Inappropriate relationships

  • Repeated or continued association, discussions (including professional ones, because frequency can look inappropriate) and sexual or amorous relations between recruits and Cape May training staff.
  • Nonprofessional association with recruits from other companies not in your week of training, unless the training staff directs it.
  • Any sexual or amorous associations or discussions between recruits.

You must report any incident of inappropriate relations. Failing to report one is a punishable offense.

Liberty

  • Liberty is time off from training. It can be limited to Training Center grounds, or your company may earn eight hours of off-base liberty in week seven.
  • Liberty is earned. If you do not meet all academic and physical fitness requirements, you will not normally get it.
  • On liberty you represent the Coast Guard. Drinking alcohol under age 21 is not permitted, and excessive drinking that causes intoxication or an inability to perform duties is punished under current policy.

Uniforms

Uniforms must be worn properly and with respect. Your conduct must reflect your pride in the Coast Guard and in your uniform.

Family information

Family, calls and mail Share with family

As a new member you become part of the Coast Guard "extended family," and so do your family members. More information is at www.uscg.mil/spouses.

Emergency contact

In an emergency, your family should contact their local American Red Cross office. For urgent questions or concerns, they can reach the Battalion Officer at (609) 898-6715, or after hours the Officer of the Day at (609) 898-6915.

Telephone calls

  • When you arrive, a letter is sent to your family to tell them you got there safely.
  • You may not receive personal phone calls during recruit training, so contact your family when you reach the Philadelphia USO.
  • Office phones at Cape May are for official business only. Your Company Commander may grant phone use in the later weeks of training.

Dependent ID cards

If you have dependents, you fill out the Application for Dependent ID Card after you arrive. The application and a letter explaining the steps are mailed to your spouse or dependent, who takes it to the nearest military installation that issues ID cards. If they cannot get there before graduation, a card can be issued at Cape May (by appointment only) or at your first duty station.

Mail

  • Mail is delivered Monday through Friday, and heavy volume can slow it down.
  • Company mail orderlies pick up mail at a time set by your Company Commander and bring it to the CC for inspection. Suspicious letters or packages are opened by you in front of your CC. Contraband is seized, so check the contraband list before anyone sends you anything.
  • You may receive letters, cards and photos that are not obscene.
  • Outgoing mail can go in the drop box at the Training Center Post Office or the mailbox between Munro Hall and the Auditorium. Mail is picked up twice a day.

Frequently asked questions

FAQ

Is there time off during training?
Yes. Time off includes divine hours on Sunday to attend worship services of your choice. In later weeks you may earn liberty for a few hours during the weekend.
What if I did not get a bank account and ATM card?
Your pay will be seriously disrupted. Take care of this before you arrive so you do not spend training time dealing with it.
How long is recruit training?
Eight weeks, unless you are held back for disciplinary, medical, academic or physical fitness reasons.
What can I receive in the mail?
Letters, cards and photos that are not obscene.
Can I get married while I am in the Coast Guard?
Yes.
How does my family reach me in an emergency?
They should contact their local Red Cross office.
Can my family pick me up at the end of recruit training?
Yes. Your family is always invited and encouraged to attend graduation.
Can I become an officer later?
Yes. Ask your unit Educational Services Officer for details.
How much leave do I get each year?
You earn 30 days of paid leave each year.

Glossary of nautical terms

Glossary

Barracks
A building where military personnel live.
Belay
(1) To make fast or secure a line. (2) To cancel an order. (3) To cease.
Below
Beneath the deck (downstairs).
Bulkhead
A partition or "wall" on a vessel.
Cabin
The Commanding Officer's living quarters.
Compartment
Enclosed spaces or "rooms" on a vessel.
Deck
The "floor" of a vessel.
Eyes in the boat
Look straight ahead at the position of attention.
Forward
Towards the bow (pronounced "forrard").
Galley
The cooking compartment or "kitchen" on a vessel.
Hatch
A square or rectangular opening in a deck.
Head
The compartment with toilet facilities; bathroom.
Heading
The direction a ship points at any given moment.
Inboard
Toward the ship's center line.
Ladder
Steps from one deck to another.
Messdeck
The compartment where enlisted personnel eat.
Muster
Assembly of the crew for accountability.
OOD
Officer of the Day/Deck. An officer in charge of a vessel or station for the day or watch.
Outboard
Out, away from the ship's center line.
Overhead
The ceiling on a vessel or compartment.
Passageway
A compartment designed for traffic, a "hallway."
Port
The left side of a vessel when looking forward.
Rack
A sailor's bed.
Scuttlebutt
(1) A drinking fountain. (2) A rumor.
Secure
Lock up, finish up.
Starboard
The right side of a vessel when looking forward.
Swab
To mop. The mop itself.
Underway
When a vessel is not tied to a pier or at anchor.
Watch
A period of duty, usually four hours.
