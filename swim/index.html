Check the standard

The test below comes from the Training Center Cape May "Helmsman" booklet (2017). Confirm the current standard with your recruiter. If you are not a confident swimmer, start now: eight weeks is enough for most people who train three days a week.

1. Jump

Jump off a six-foot platform into the pool.

2. Swim 100 meters

Swimming on your back is not permitted. Breaststroke, freestyle and sidestroke are the usual choices.

3. Tread water

Stay afloat for 5 minutes.

Train 3 days a week

Three sessions of 30 to 45 minutes, with a rest day between. Rest days are when you improve.

The basics

The test you are training for Standard

PartWhat you do
JumpJump off a six-foot platform into the pool.
SwimSwim 100 meters. Swimming on your back is not permitted.
TreadTread water for 5 minutes.
  • The booklet gives no time limit for the 100 meters. One recruit's account says instructors want continuous forward movement and describes the standard as 100 meters in about 5 minutes. Aim for under 4 minutes so you have margin either way.
  • Freestyle, breaststroke and sidestroke are all accepted. Choose the stroke you can keep going the longest, not the fastest one.
  • Recruits who miss the swim go to remedial swim sessions, which start earlier in the morning. It is far better to arrive already comfortable.
  • Speed is not the goal. Being relaxed and comfortable in the water is.

Gear and pool safety Setup

  • Suit: a snug training suit. Loose suits create drag.
  • Goggles: put the eyepieces on first, then pull the strap over your head. They should seal on the soft tissue around your eyes without pressing on bone.
  • Cap: optional unless your hair is long. Wet your hair first.
  • Kickboard and pull buoy: useful for isolating kick and arms. Fins and paddles are optional and can wait.
  • Rinse gear in fresh water after each use.

Safety

  • Swim only where a lifeguard is on duty, and never train alone in open water.
  • If you are nervous, use a lane next to the wall and rest at the wall as often as you need.
  • Shower before you get in, and enter feet first after checking that the water is clear.
  • Learn circle swimming in shared lanes: stay on the right side of the lane and go counterclockwise.
  • Stop if you feel dizzy, cramped or very out of breath. Rest, hydrate and try again later.

Getting comfortable in water Week 1

Fear and tension waste more energy than any bad stroke. Spend the first week making the water feel normal.

  1. Bubbles. In shallow water, hold the wall, put your face in and exhale steadily for 3 to 5 seconds. Lift, breathe in, repeat 10 times.
  2. Float your feet. Hold the wall with both hands and lift your feet off the bottom. Repeat the bubbles while floating, with a light kick.
  3. Front float. Take a breath, put your face in, and stretch out like a starfish. Count how long you float. Stand when you need to.
  4. Push and glide. Push off the wall in a streamlined position, arms stretched overhead, face down. Glide as far as you can before you stand.
  5. Recover to standing. Practice pulling your knees to your chest and putting your feet down, so you are never stuck.
Tip

Relax. Tight muscles sink and tire quickly. A slow, full exhale underwater is the most important habit you will learn.

TechniqueLearn freestyle for speed and breathing control, breaststroke for resting, and a good treading kick. You only need one stroke for the test, but knowing two makes it safe.

Freestyle Stroke

Body position

  • Lie flat with your head, hips and feet near the surface, like a torpedo.
  • Look at the bottom, slightly forward. Press your head and chest down a little to lift your hips.
  • Rotate your whole body side to side from the shoulders instead of bending at the waist.

Kick

  • Keep it small and relaxed. Loose ankles, heels just under the surface.
  • A bigger kick adds drag, not speed. For a beginner, the kick mostly keeps your legs up.

Arms

  • Reach forward, press your hand and forearm back toward your feet, then recover your elbow over the surface.
  • One arm extends as the other pulls. Keep a bit of glide to stay balanced.

Beginner order of learning

  1. Kick on your front with a board, face in, until you can do 2 lengths.
  2. Add one arm at a time (one-arm drill) with the other arm extended.
  3. Add side breathing (see Breathing).
  4. Put it together at an easy pace and count strokes per length.

Breathing Key skill

The rule

Exhale fully underwater, then turn to breathe in. Never hold your breath and then try to exhale and inhale at the surface. That is why beginners run out of air.

Four-step breathing progression

  1. Blowing bubbles. Hold the wall, face in, exhale for 3 to 5 seconds, lift to inhale.
  2. Floating feet. Same, but with your feet lifted and a light kick.
  3. Add one arm stroke. Hold the wall, exhale, take one stroke, and turn your head to the side of that arm to inhale as the arm leaves the water by your hip. Return your face as the hand returns to the wall.
  4. Add a kickboard. Hold the board with arms extended, kick lightly, take one stroke and turn to breathe. Try a few lengths, alternating sides.
  • Keep the arm opposite to your breathing side extended while you turn.
  • Keep gliding while you breathe. If you stop, you sink.
  • Once it feels natural, breathe every 3 strokes to alternate sides. If that feels hard, breathe every 2 strokes.
  • If you are very out of breath, breathe every stroke and slow down.

Breaststroke Stroke

The best stroke for a beginner who needs to rest while moving. Your face can stay up, and the glide gives you a break on every stroke.

Think of it as three beats: pull, kick, glide.

  1. Streamline. Start with arms extended, legs together, head neutral.
  2. Pull. Sweep your hands out just past shoulder width, bend your elbows so the forearms face back, and pull to about nose level. Keep elbows high.
  3. Breathe. Lift your head to breathe as your hands reach the front of your chest. Exhale underwater before.
  4. Kick. Cue: up, out, around, together. Pull your heels up toward your hips, keep your knees no wider than your shoulders, press your feet out and around, then snap them together.
  5. Glide. Shoot your hands forward into streamline and hold for a 2-count before the next stroke.

Timing rule: arms pull, then legs kick as the arms shoot forward. Do not pull and kick at the same time.

Sidestroke Option

Sidestroke lets you keep your face above water on the side. The military combat version is the same idea: a single kick and a long glide.

  1. Lie on your side, head up, bottom arm extended, top hand near your chest.
  2. Pull with the top arm back to your thigh while the bottom arm sweeps in a short scull.
  3. Kick once with a scissor kick as you pull, then glide in a long, streamlined position.
  • Most common mistake: kicking constantly like freestyle. That tires you and slows every length.
  • Another: skipping the glide. The glide is where you rest.

Treading water Test part 3

Technique

  • Body: stay upright, back straight, knees bent as if sitting in a chair.
  • Legs (eggbeater kick): bend your knees toward your chest at about 90 degrees. Rotate one foot clockwise and the other counterclockwise, moving only from the knee down. Your feet should never meet. A breaststroke-style kick also works.
  • Arms (sculling): hold your arms out to the sides like a T. Keep fingers together, hands gently cupped, elbows slightly bent. Sweep your hands horizontally just below the surface, pushing water out and pulling it back.
  • Breathing: breathe calmly through your mouth. Do not gasp.

How to last 5 minutes

  • Think marathon, not sprint. Relax your shoulders and keep your kick and sculling small.
  • If your arms tire, rest them on your chest and let your legs do the work for a few seconds, then go back.
  • Stay near the pool edge while you learn, then move to deeper water.

The jump Test part 1

The test starts with a jump from a six-foot platform. Practice the same entry, even from a lower height.

  1. Look straight ahead at the horizon, not down at the water.
  2. Take a breath, then step or jump out. Keep your feet together and your legs straight.
  3. Cross your arms over your chest (or cover your nose with one hand and hold your wrist with the other).
  4. When you surface, start kicking and head for the wall or swim lane.
  • Practice stepping into deep water from the deck, then from a low board if the pool has one and a lifeguard approves.
  • Surface calmly, take a breath and start swimming. Your first strokes after the jump are where beginners panic.

These steps are general good practice and are not from the Helmsman booklet. Ask your lifeguard or instructor to check your jump.

The planThree sessions a week with a rest day in between. Take as much rest as you need between sets. In the tables, "length" means one trip across the pool.

8-week training plan Weeks 1 to 8

Every session has the same shape

Warm-up (5 minutes of easy swimming, kicking or treading) → skill work → main set → treading → cool-down (easy swimming or floating). Total 30 to 45 minutes.

Week 1: comfort

SessionPlan
ABubbles 10 × 5 sec. Front float 6 times. Push and glide 6 times. Tread 3 × 30 sec in shallow water.
BFloating feet with bubbles 8 times. Kick with a board for half a length, 4 times. Tread 3 × 30 sec.
CRepeat A and B. Try 1 length of kick with a board without stopping.

Week 2: kick and basic freestyle

SessionPlan
AKick with a board: 4 × 1 length. Breathing drill 3 (one stroke + turn) at the wall, 6 each side. Tread 3 × 1 min.
BKick with a board and side breathing: 4 × 1 length. One-arm drill: 4 × half length. Tread 3 × 1 min.
CFreestyle with rests: 6 × half length. Kick: 2 × 1 length. Tread 3 × 1 min.

Week 3: breathing and breaststroke

SessionPlan
AFreestyle: 4 × 1 length, 30 sec rest. Breathing every 3 strokes. Tread 2 × 1:30.
BBreaststroke kick on the wall and with a board: 4 × 1 length. Breaststroke: 4 × half length, 2-count glide. Tread 2 × 1:30.
CChoose your best stroke: 6 × 1 length, 30 sec rest. Try 1 length without stopping. Tread 2 × 1:30.

Week 4: endurance

SessionPlan
A4 × 2 lengths, 45 sec rest. Tread 2 × 2 min.
BFreestyle 4 × 1 length, then breaststroke 4 × 1 length, 20 sec rest. Tread 2 × 2 min.
CSwim 2 lengths without stopping, 3 times, rest 1 min. Tread 2 × 2 min.

Week 5: distance

SessionPlan
A3 × 3 lengths, 45 sec rest. Tread 2 × 3 min.
BLadder: 1, 2, 3, 2, 1 lengths, 20 sec rest between. Tread 2 × 3 min.
CSwim 3 lengths nonstop, then rest 1 min, then 2 more lengths. Tread 2 × 3 min.

Week 6: first full 100 meters

SessionPlan
A3 × 4 lengths, 1 min rest. Tread 1 × 4 min.
B5 × 2 lengths at an easy pace, 15 sec rest. Tread 1 × 4 min.
CBenchmark: swim 100 m (4 lengths of a 25 m pool or 5 of a 25 yd pool) without stopping. Write down your time. Tread 1 × 4 min.

Week 7: test simulation

SessionPlan
ASwim 100 m nonstop, rest 2 min, swim 100 m nonstop. Tread 1 × 5 min.
BSkill: jumps from the deck (3). Then 2 × 50 m at goal pace, 20 sec rest, then 1 × 100 m easy. Tread 1 × 5 min.
CSwim 150 m nonstop (6 lengths of a 25 m pool). Tread 1 × 5 min, with no wall touches.

Week 8: sharpen and mock test

SessionPlan
AEasy technique day: 6 × 50 m easy with 20 sec rest. Tread 1 × 3 min. Practice jumps.
BEasy: 4 × 50 m, plus 1 × 100 m at goal pace. Tread 1 × 2 min. Rest tomorrow.
CMock test: jump from the deck or a platform, swim 100 m nonstop with no backstroke, get out and rest briefly, then tread 5 minutes. Do the swim in the same conditions as test day.

If you cannot finish a week's targets, repeat that week before moving on. It is better to take 10 weeks than to rush.

Pace and lap times Improve

The test is not scored by time, but the same work that makes you faster also makes the swim feel easier. Use these tools to measure it.

Count your strokes

Count the strokes per length. Fewer strokes at the same effort means a longer, more efficient stroke. Aim to bring your count down over the 8 weeks.

Time one length and one 100

  • Use the pool's pace clock, a waterproof watch or a phone timer on the deck.
  • Record your 100 m benchmark in week 6 and again in week 8.
  • A realistic beginner goal: finish 100 m in under 4:00 without stopping.

Training sets for endurance

  • Short rests: start with longer rests and shorter sets, then reduce rest over time.
  • 50-50 swim: 50 m freestyle, then 50 m of your test stroke, rest 10 to 15 seconds. Build to 10 to 15 rounds.
  • Goal-pace 50s: swim 50 m at the pace you want for the test, repeat 5 to 10 times, with 10 to 15 seconds of rest. A 54-second 50 m pace is about a 1:48 per 100 m.
  • Pyramid: 1, 2, 3, 4 lengths, then back down, with short rests.
  • Interval progression: once you can do 100s, set an interval pace for your swims and add one more 100 each week.
Rule of thumb

If you are gasping at the end of a set, slow down. You should be able to hold a short conversation after an easy length. The pace you can sustain is the pace you build on.

Test day

Test day and if you miss Checklist

  • Before: sleep well, eat a light meal a couple of hours earlier, and drink water.
  • Warm up: stretch your shoulders and legs, and get in the water if allowed.
  • Jump: look ahead, feet together, arms crossed. Surface and start kicking.
  • Swim: start slower than you think you need. Use your best stroke and breathe with a steady rhythm. If you need a rest, glide on the breaststroke. Do not stop and do not swim on your back.
  • Tread: use the eggbeater kick, keep your sculling small, and breathe calmly.

If you miss

  • You will be put in remedial swim, which starts before the normal wake-up. It is common and fixable.
  • Listen to the instructor, and focus on relaxing in the water and keeping your face down.

After the testOnce you can swim 100 m and tread for 5 minutes, this is how to build real endurance: swim farther, recover faster, and stay relaxed when you are tired.

Endurance principles Start here

Endurance is efficiency plus patience

Swimmers rarely tire because their lungs or legs give out first. They tire because they waste energy: a high head, a hard kick, a rushed stroke. Fix efficiency first, then add distance.

  • Change one thing at a time. Add distance, or shorten rest, or swim faster. Never all three in the same week.
  • Keep three sessions a week with a rest day between, as in the 8-week plan. Add distance inside those sessions before adding more sessions.
  • Increase total distance by about 10 to 15% a week. Every fourth week, drop back to about 70% to recover.
  • Most of the work should feel easy. You should be able to speak a short sentence at the wall. Save hard efforts for one set a week.
  • Track it. Write down distance, rest and how it felt. Progress you can see keeps you going.

  • Count your strokes per length. A rising count means you are tiring. Slow down and lengthen the stroke.

Long, slow swims The base

  • Once a week, swim continuously at an easy pace. Start with 200 m, then add 50 m each week.
  • Pick a comfortable pace you can hold. If you must gasp, slow down. If you can talk easily, speed up a little.
  • Breathe every 3 strokes when you can, and exhale fully underwater so you never hold your breath.
  • Mix strokes if you need a break. Swim freestyle, then a length of breaststroke or sidestroke, then freestyle again. Do not stop.
  • Long swims prepare you for the unexpected. A swim that feels easy in training is a swim that is safe on a bad day.

Swim more efficiently Do more with less

  • Glide. Let each stroke carry you. Pause a moment with your arm extended before you pull again.
  • Look down. A neutral head keeps your hips high and cuts drag.
  • Kick small. Kick from the hips with loose ankles. A big kick burns energy and adds little speed.
  • Rotate your body. Roll your hips and shoulders together on each stroke. It makes breathing easy and gives you a longer reach.
  • Pull under your body, not out to the side. Keep your elbow high and push the water back.
  • Stroke-count drill: swim 4 lengths and count strokes on each. Try to lower the count by one without swimming faster.

Treading for longer Beyond 5 minutes

  • Build the time. Move from 5 minutes to 6, 8 and then 10 minutes in steps. Rest 1 minute between sets.
  • Hands out of the water. Tread 1 minute with your hands above your head, then 1 minute sculling. Rest and repeat. It trains your legs to do the work.
  • Use the eggbeater kick. It gives steady lift with less effort than a scissor kick. Keep your sculling small and your breathing calm.
  • Stay tall and relaxed. Keep your head up and shoulders loose. Panic wastes more energy than anything else.

Dry-land support Twice a week

  • Shoulders and back: band pull-aparts, rows and push-ups, 2 sets of 10 to 15 each.
  • Core: planks and dead bugs, 3 × 30 sec. A strong core keeps your hips up in the water.
  • Legs: squats and lunges for kicking power, 2 sets of 12.
  • Mobility: shoulder and ankle stretches after every session so your reach and kick stay loose.
  • Sleep, food and water matter as much as pool time. Eat a light meal 2 hours before you swim and drink water after.
