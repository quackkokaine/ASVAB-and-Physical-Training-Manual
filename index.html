Overview and study planTwo ASVAB sections test math, and both count toward your AFQT score, which decides whether you qualify to enlist. No calculator is allowed on either one.

SectionWhat it testsTypical format*
Arithmetic Reasoning (AR)Word problems: percentages, rates, ratios, interest, averages, probabilityComputer: 15 questions, 55 min. Paper: 30 questions, 36 min.

*Computer figures are the scored questions on the official ASVAB site; the computer test can add unscored tryout questions, which lengthens the time limit. Paper figures are the commonly published ones. Confirm your version with your recruiter.

Your AFQT is built from AR + MK + 2 × Verbal Expression, so math is half of the score that matters most.

Computer version
  • You cannot go back, so work each problem carefully and check it before you submit.
  • Time is generous. Use it to read slowly, set up the equation, and double-check arithmetic.
  • If you are stuck, eliminate what you can, make your best guess, and move on.
  • Finish before time runs out: unfinished questions are scored as if guessed at random.
Paper version
  • Pace yourself: about a minute per question, a little more on word problems.
  • Skip a hard question, mark it, and come back after the easy ones.
  • Answer every question before time is called, since a blank cannot earn a point.
  • Do your work in the space provided and recopy answers carefully onto the answer sheet.

Either way: no calculator is allowed, so the mental-math practice in this guide applies to both versions.

Mental math strategies

Key ideas

  • Break apart: split a number into friendly pieces. 7 × 48 = 7 × 50 − 7 × 2 = 350 − 14 = 336.
  • Round and adjust: 198 + 47 = 200 + 47 − 2 = 245.
  • Halve and double: 16 × 25 = 8 × 50 = 400. Think of 25 as a quarter of 100: 36 × 25 = 36 ÷ 4 × 100 = 900.
  • Multiply by 5: halve it, then multiply by 10. 46 × 5 = 23 × 10 = 230.
  • Multiply by 9: multiply by 10, then subtract the number. 9 × 37 = 370 − 37 = 333.
  • Multiply a two-digit number by 11: when the digits add to 9 or less, put their sum in the middle. 11 × 34 = 3 (3 + 4) 4 = 374.
  • Square a number ending in 5: multiply the tens digit by one more than itself, then write 25. 35² = (3 × 4) then 25 = 1,225.
  • Shift the decimal: multiplying or dividing by 10, 100, or 1,000 just moves the decimal point. 0.45 × 1,000 = 450.
  • Estimate first: round the numbers to find the neighborhood of the answer and throw out choices that are far off.

Long multiplication and division

Key ideas

  • Multiply with partial products: multiply by the ones digit, then by the tens digit (put a zero in the ones place first), then add the rows.
  • Check by estimating: 47 × 36 is close to 45 × 40 = 1,800, so 1,692 is reasonable.
  • Long division is a loop: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down the next digit, and repeat.
  • Check division by multiplying back: quotient × divisor + remainder = the number you started with.

Arithmetic ReasoningMost AR questions are one or two steps once you translate the words into an equation. Underline what is asked, write the numbers, then choose the operation.

Fractions AR

Key ideas

  • Add or subtract: find a common denominator, then add or subtract the tops. 13 + 14 = 412 + 312 = 712
  • Multiply: top times top, bottom times bottom. Cancel common factors first to keep the numbers small. 23 × 38 = 624 = 14
  • Divide: flip the second fraction, then multiply. 34 ÷ 38 = 34 × 83 = 2412 = 2
  • Simplify: divide the top and bottom by the same number. 1824 = 34 (divide by 6)
  • Mixed number to improper fraction: multiply the whole number by the bottom, then add the top. 325 = 175
  • Fraction of a number: of means multiply. 35 of 40 = 35 × 40 = 24
  • Compare fractions: cross-multiply. The side with the larger product is the larger fraction. 34 vs 57: 3 × 7 = 21 and 5 × 4 = 20, so 34 is larger

Percentages AR

Key ideas

Percent formulapart = percent × whole
  • Convert by moving the decimal two places: 35% = 0.35.
  • Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. A price rising from 40 to 50 is 10 ÷ 40 = 25%.
  • Discount: pay (100 − discount)% of the price. A 20% discount on 60 costs 0.80 × 60 = 48.

Watch (Khan Academy): Percent word problem example 4

Extra tips

  • "Is over of" setup: part ÷ whole = percent ÷ 100, so 18 is 30% of 60 because 18 ÷ 60 = 30 ÷ 100.
  • Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. Always divide by the old value.
  • Shortcuts: 10% is one decimal place left, 5% is half of that, and 15% is 10% + 5%.
  • Successive percent changes multiply, they don't add. 20% off and then 10% off leaves 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72 of the price, which is 28% off, not 30%.
  • Sales tax and tips: multiply by (1 + rate). A price with 8% tax is price × 1.08.

Worked examples

A $80 jacket is marked down 25%, then 10% off the sale price. Final price: 80 × 0.75 = 60, then 60 × 0.90 = $54.

18 is 30% of what number? 18 ÷ 0.30 = 60.

Try it

  1. What is 15% of 240?
  2. A population grows from 400 to 460. What is the percent increase?
  3. A $50 item has 8% sales tax. What is the total?
  4. 45 is what percent of 60?
  5. After a 20% discount, a coat costs $48. What was the original price?

Ratios and proportions AR

Key ideas

  • A ratio of 3:5 means parts of 3 and 5, so 8 parts in all.
  • Solve proportions by cross-multiplying: ab = cd gives a × d = b × c. For example x6 = 1015 gives 15x = 60, so x = 4.

Watch (Khan Academy): Setting up proportions to solve word problems · Ratio word problem examples

Extra tips

  • Part-to-part ratios (boys to girls, 3:4) add up to the whole (7 parts). Part-to-whole ratios compare one group to the total.
  • Unit rate: divide to find the cost or amount of one item, then multiply. 5 pens for $3.75 is $0.75 per pen.
  • Keep units matching on both sides of a proportion (miles over hours = miles over hours).
  • Scale drawings and maps are proportions: multiply the inches by 25 miles per inch.

Worked examples

Paint is mixed 2 parts blue to 3 parts white. How much blue is in 15 gallons? There are 5 parts, so each part is 3 gallons, and blue is 2 × 3 = 6 gallons.

A car uses 6 gallons for 150 miles. How many gallons for 400 miles? 150 ÷ 6 = 25 miles per gallon, and 400 ÷ 25 = 16 gallons.

  4. Solve x9 = 812
  5. A map scale is 1 inch = 40 miles. Two towns are 234 inches apart. How many miles apart are they?

Rate, time, and distance AR

Key ideas

Distanced = r × t
  • A car at 55 mph for 3 hours travels 165 miles.
  • Two travelers moving toward each other: add their speeds. Moving the same direction: subtract.
  • Work problems: add rates. If A finishes a job in 4 hours and B in 12, add their hourly rates: 14 + 112 = 312 + 112 = 412 = 13 of the job per hour, so together they take 3 hours.

Watch (Khan Academy): Rate problem using fractions

Extra tips

  • Average speed is total distance ÷ total time, not the average of the two speeds.
  • Catch-up problems: the closing speed is the difference of the speeds, and the gap is the head start.
  • Work problems: add rates. Combined time = (a × b) ÷ (a + b) for two workers who take a and b hours alone.
  • Boats and planes: downstream or with the wind, add the current; upstream or against it, subtract.
  • Convert minutes to hours before using mph: 45 minutes = 0.75 hour.

Worked examples

A driver goes 60 miles at 30 mph and 60 miles back at 60 mph. Average speed? Time is 2 + 1 = 3 hours for 120 miles, so 120 ÷ 3 = 40 mph (not 45).

A truck leaves at 60 mph. A car leaves the same place 1 hour later at 75 mph. How long until the car catches up? The truck has a 60-mile head start and the car closes 15 miles per hour, so 60 ÷ 15 = 4 hours.

  1. A train goes 210 miles in 3.5 hours. What is its speed?
  2. Two cyclists start 90 miles apart and ride toward each other at 12 mph and 18 mph. When do they meet?
  3. A runner goes 6 mph for 45 minutes. How far does she run?
  4. Machine A packs a batch in 10 hours and machine B in 15 hours. How long together?
  5. A boat goes 24 miles downstream in 2 hours and returns in 3 hours. What is the speed of the current?

Simple interest AR

Key ideas

Simple interestI = P × r × t
  • P is the starting amount, r is the yearly rate as a decimal, t is years. 500 at 6% for 3 years earns 500 × 0.06 × 3 = 90.

Watch (Khan Academy): Introduction to interest (covers simple vs. compound)

Extra tips

  • Time must be in years: 18 months = 1.5 years, 9 months = 0.75 year, 90 days is about 0.25 year.
  • Total owed or earned = principal + interest.
  • Rearrange I = P × r × t to find any missing piece: r = I ÷ (P × t), t = I ÷ (P × r), P = I ÷ (r × t).
  • Compound interest earns interest on interest: A = P(1 + r)t. The ASVAB mostly tests simple interest, but know the difference.

Worked examples

$1,500 at 5% simple interest for 18 months. I = 1,500 × 0.05 × 1.5 = $112.50.

How long does it take $800 to earn $96 at 4%? t = 96 ÷ (800 × 0.04) = 96 ÷ 32 = 3 years.

Try it

  2. Interest on $500 at 6% for 9 months.
  3. What rate does $600 need to earn $90 in 3 years?
  4. Total owed on a $1,000 loan at 8% simple interest for 2 years.
  5. What principal earns $120 in 2 years at 4%?

Averages AR

Key ideas

Meanmean = sumnumber of values
  • Median: the middle value after sorting (average the two middle values if the count is even).
  • Mode: the most frequent value.
  • Missing value: needed total = target average × count. To average 80 over 5 tests with scores of 72, 85, 90, 78, you need 400 − 325 = 75 on the last one.

Watch (Khan Academy): Finding mean, median, and mode

Extra tips

  • Range = highest − lowest. It measures spread, not center.
  • Weighted average: multiply each average by its group size, add, then divide by the total count.
  • Adding a value above the mean raises the mean; adding one below lowers it.
  • A data set can have no mode, one mode, or several.

Worked examples

Scores are 82, 90, 76, and 88. What score on the fifth test gives an average of 85? Needed total = 5 × 85 = 425. Current total = 336. Fifth score = 89.

Class A has 20 students averaging 80 and class B has 30 averaging 90. Combined average = (20 × 80 + 30 × 90) ÷ 50 = 4,300 ÷ 50 = 86.

  2. Median of 12, 3, 9, 7, 20, 15.
  3. The mean of five numbers is 12. Four of them are 10, 11, 13, and 14. What is the fifth?
  4. Range of 14, 3, 22, 9.
  5. Mode of 5, 7, 7, 9, 5, 7.

Probability AR

Key ideas

ProbabilityP = favorable outcomestotal outcomes
  • Independent events: multiply the probabilities.
  • Without replacement: reduce the total after each draw.
Examples
A bag has 3 red and 5 blue marbles. P(red) = 38
Two heads in a row on a fair coin: 12 × 12 = 14
Two red marbles without replacement: 38 × 27 = 656 = 328

Watch (Khan Academy): Probability explained

Extra tips

  • Probability is always between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain).
  • Complement rule: P(not A) = 1 − P(A). If P(rain) = 0.3, P(no rain) = 0.7.
  • "Or" for events that can't both happen: add. "And" for independent events: multiply.
  • Counting principle: multiply the choices at each step. 5 shirts × 4 pants × 2 hats = 40 outfits.
  • Arrangements of n different items in a line = n! (n factorial). 4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24.

Worked examples

A die is rolled. P(even) = 36 = 12.

A bag has 4 red and 6 green marbles. Draw one: P(green) = 610 = 35. Draw two without replacement: P(both green) = 610 × 59 = 3090 = 13.

  2. P(rain) is 0.3. What is P(no rain)?
  3. A fair coin is flipped 3 times. What is the probability of all heads?
  4. How many outfits from 5 shirts, 4 pants, and 2 hats?
  5. In how many ways can 4 people stand in a line?

Mathematics KnowledgeMK questions are short and direct: algebra, exponents, radicals, and geometry. Memorize the rules and formulas below, then practice for speed.

Order of operations (PEMDAS) MK

Key ideas

  • Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication and Division (left to right), Addition and Subtraction (left to right). 3 + 4 × 2² = 3 + 4 × 4 = 19.

Extra tips

  • Multiplication and division share a rank, as do addition and subtraction. Work each pair left to right.
  • Exponents bind tighter than a minus sign: −4² = −16, but (−4)² = 16.
  • A fraction bar groups like parentheses: simplify the top and the bottom separately, then divide.
  • Work inside the innermost parentheses first.

Number properties MK

Key ideas

  • Prime numbers have exactly two factors: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23.
  • GCF is the largest number dividing both (12 and 18: 6). LCM is the smallest shared multiple (12 and 18: 36).
  • Scientific notation: 4,500 = 4.5 × 10³ and 0.003 = 3 × 10⁻³.

Extra tips

  • Divisibility: by 2 if the last digit is even; by 3 if the digits add to a multiple of 3; by 5 if it ends in 0 or 5; by 9 if the digits add to a multiple of 9; by 10 if it ends in 0.
  • Prime factorization breaks a number into primes: 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5. Use it to find the GCF (shared primes, lowest powers) and the LCM (all primes, highest powers).
  • Even + even = even, odd + odd = even, even + odd = odd. Even × anything = even.
  • Consecutive integers go up by 1 (n, n + 1, n + 2). Consecutive even or odd integers go up by 2.
  • Absolute value is distance from zero: |−7| = 7.

Worked examples

Find the GCF and LCM of 24 and 36. 24 = 2³ × 3 and 36 = 2² × 3². GCF = 2² × 3 = 12. LCM = 2³ × 3² = 72.

Is 4,572 divisible by 9? The digits add to 4 + 5 + 7 + 2 = 18, which is a multiple of 9, so yes.

Solving equations MK

Key ideas

  • Do the same thing to both sides until the variable is alone. 3x − 7 = 14 gives 3x = 21, so x = 7.
  • Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number flips the sign: −2x < 8 gives x > −4.

Watch (Khan Academy): Solving two-step equations

Extra tips

  • Variables on both sides: collect the variable terms on one side first, then isolate it.
  • Distribute before solving: 4(x − 2) = 4x − 8.
  • Fractions: multiply every term by the common denominator to clear them.
  • Inequalities flip the sign when you multiply or divide by a negative number.
  • Translating words: "3 more than twice a number" is 2n + 3. "5 less than a number" is n − 5.

Exponent rules MK

Three rulesxa × xb = xa+bxa ÷ xb = xa−b(xa)b = xab
Two special casesx0 = 1 (x ≠ 0)x−n = 1xn

Key ideas

  • Same base, multiply: add the exponents.
  • Same base, divide: subtract the exponents.
  • Power of a power: multiply the exponents.
  • Zero power: anything (except 0) to the zero power is 1.
  • Negative exponent: one over the same power, so 4−2 is 116.

  • Powers of 10 shift the decimal point: 4.5 × 10³ = 4,500.

Factoring and FOIL MK

Key ideas

FOIL(x + a)(x + b) = x² + (a + b)x + ab
Difference of squaresa² − b² = (a + b)(a − b)

Extra tips

Angles MK

Key ideas

  • Complementary angles sum to 90°. Supplementary angles sum to 180°.
  • Angles in a triangle sum to 180°; in a quadrilateral, 360°.
  • Vertical angles (opposite each other where two lines cross) are equal.
  • When a line crosses two parallel lines, corresponding angles are equal and co-interior angles sum to 180°.

Watch (Khan Academy): Complementary and supplementary angles · Vertical, adjacent and linearly paired angles

Extra tips

  • When a line crosses two parallel lines, corresponding angles are equal and alternate interior angles are equal.
  • An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.
  • The interior angles of an n-sided polygon add up to (n − 2) × 180°. A regular polygon splits that total evenly.
  • Angles around a point add to 360°, and angles on a straight line add to 180°.

Worked examples

The angles of a triangle are 2x, 3x, and 4x. Then 9x = 180, so x = 20 and the angles are 40°, 60°, and 80°.

Each angle of a regular pentagon: (5 − 2) × 180 = 540, and 540 ÷ 5 = 108°.

Right triangles MK

Key ideas

Pythagorean theorema² + b² = c²
  • c is the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle. Memorize the triples 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, and their multiples (6-8-10, 9-12-15). For special triangles: in a 45-45-90, the hypotenuse is the leg × √2; in a 30-60-90, the sides are x, x√3, and 2x.

Watch (Khan Academy): Introduction to the Pythagorean theorem

Extra tips

  • To find a leg instead of the hypotenuse, rearrange: a² = c² − b².
  • The diagonal of a rectangle is the hypotenuse of a right triangle made by its length and width.
  • 45-45-90 triangle: legs equal, hypotenuse = leg × √2.
  • 30-60-90 triangle: the sides are x, x√3, and 2x, with the short leg opposite the 30° angle.
  • If a² + b² = c², the triangle is a right triangle.

A 10-foot ladder leans on a wall with its base 6 feet from the wall. How high does it reach? Height = √(10² − 6²) = √64 = 8 feet.

The diagonal of a 9 by 12 rectangle: √(81 + 144) = √225 = 15.

Triangle types MK

Key ideas

  • Equilateral: three equal sides, three 60° angles.
  • Isosceles: two equal sides and two equal angles.
  • Similar triangles have equal angles and proportional sides, so set up a proportion to find a missing side.

Extra tips

  • Classify by sides (equilateral, isosceles, scalene) and by angles (acute, right, obtuse).
  • Triangle inequality: any two sides must add to more than the third. So sides a and b allow a third side between |a − b| and a + b.
  • Similar triangles have equal angles and proportional sides. Find the scale factor and multiply.
  • The longest side is opposite the largest angle.

Worked examples

Can sides 3, 4, and 8 make a triangle? 3 + 4 = 7, which is less than 8, so no.

Coordinate geometry MK

Key ideas

Slopem = y₂ − y₁x₂ − x₁
Line equationy = mx + b
  • m is the slope and b is where the line crosses the y-axis. Parallel lines share a slope; perpendicular lines have slopes that are negative reciprocals: a line with slope 2 is perpendicular to a line with slope −12.

Watch (Khan Academy): Introduction to slope

Extra tips

The midpoint of a segment is the average of the coordinates:

Midpoint(x₁ + x₂2, y₁ + y₂2)

The distance between two points comes from the Pythagorean theorem:

Distanced = √(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²
  • Parallel lines have the same slope. Perpendicular lines have slopes that multiply to −1.
  • A horizontal line has slope 0. A vertical line has an undefined slope.
  • To read slope and intercept from an equation, solve for y: 3x + y = 7 becomes y = −3x + 7, so the slope is −3 and the y-intercept is 7.

Test-day strategy and cheat sheet

Strategy

  • On the computer-adaptive test you cannot skip or go back, so answer every question and never leave one blank.
  • Estimate first: if the answer must be near 50 and two choices are 5 and 500, they are out.
  • Plug answer choices back into the problem when the algebra feels slow.
  • Watch units: a problem in minutes with an answer choice in hours is a common trap.
  • Read the last sentence of a word problem first so you know what is being asked.
  • Sleep well and eat before the test; pacing matters more than speed.
  • On the paper test you can skip a hard question and return to it before time runs out. Mark it so you can find it.
  • Paper pacing: about 1.2 minutes per Arithmetic Reasoning question and about 1 minute per Mathematics Knowledge question.
